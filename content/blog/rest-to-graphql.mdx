## The breaking point

For the first two years our app's API was a perfectly reasonable REST setup. Resources, verbs, sensible status codes. Then the mobile team asked for a screen that needed fragments of seven resources at once.

We did what any reasonable team would do: we built a "summary" endpoint. Then another. Then another. By the time I noticed, we had **fourteen** bespoke aggregation endpoints, each subtly different, all written by exhausted humans at 2am.

## Why GraphQL won, eventually

It wasn't the schema. It wasn't the tooling. It was, embarrassingly, **the social contract** GraphQL introduced.

- Frontend asks for exactly what it needs.
- Backend documents what's possible.
- The conversation between teams stops being "please add a field to the summary endpoint" and starts being "is the schema correct?"

## What I miss about REST

- Cacheability. HTTP caching is a real superpower we gave up.
- Predictable cost per request.
- The ability to read a request URL and know what it does.

## What I don't miss

- Bespoke aggregation endpoints.
- The argument over PATCH semantics.
- Versioning.

## Would I do it again?

For an app with a single backend and multiple varied clients, yes — instantly. For an internal microservice talking to one other microservice, no. REST is still better there. Pick the tool, not the trend.
